Script 1: Ilia Topuria comeback
Source file: script.txt
INTRO
Ilia Topuria racked up 17 wins without a single loss, knocked out three former champions in a row, and became the first undefeated two-division champion the UFC had ever seen. Then on June 14th, 2026, on the South Lawn of the White House, with an estimated 34 million people watching around the world, his own corner told him the fight was over.
His record now sits at 17 and 1, with no fight booked and no opponent named. Dana White told reporters on November 4th, quote, \"He'll come back in early 2027 and we're working on that too.\" What separates the old Topuria from whatever version returns involves broken facial bones, a baseless glove tampering accusation, a coaching split after 13 years, and a personal life that unraveled in public. The guy walking back into the Octagon carries all of that with him now.
This whole story deserves a proper telling, because it's one of the wildest arcs in recent UFC history.
FROM REFUGEE KID TO KNOCKOUT ARTIST
Topuria entered the world on January 21st, 1997, in the German city of Halle, born to Georgian refugees who had fled the conflict in Abkhazia. By seven, his family had relocated back to Georgia, and that's where he picked up Greco-Roman wrestling. When the 2008 Russo-Georgian War forced them to move again, they landed in Alicante, Spain, and he was fifteen years old at the time.
Three countries before he could drive a car, raised by a refugee family with no stability, and this is the kid who grew up to become a UFC champion representing both Georgia and Spain, the first from either country to hold a belt.
What most casual fans don't realize is that Topuria built his foundation as a grappler, not a striker. A silver medal at the 2014 IBJJF European Championships as a junior put him on the map early, and by 2018, he and his older brother Aleksandre had earned the distinction of becoming the first Georgian Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu black belts, training under Jorge and Agustín Climent at Climent Club in Alicante. Eight submissions sit on his record because the guy could always wrestle and finish people on the ground.
What happened next changed everything about his career. Topuria rebuilt himself from scratch into a pressure boxer, modeling his body work after Canelo Álvarez's Mexican style, incorporating a shoulder roll on defense, and developing one-punch power that terrified everyone at 145 and 155 pounds. Before the Gaethje fight, his numbers painted the picture clearly: 4.81 significant strikes landed per minute, roughly 94 to 95 percent takedown defense, and 61 percent takedown accuracy, which meant he could hurt you standing and control you on the ground whenever he wanted. Seven straight fights with a knockdown in them tied the all-time UFC record.
Merab Dvalishvili once admitted that Topuria dropped him twice with body shots during sparring, which tells you how much power this man carries even when he's not trying to finish someone.
Standing five foot eight with a 69-inch reach and an orthodox stance, fighting under the nickname \"El Matador,\" Topuria speaks Georgian, Spanish, English, and some Mingrelian, a product of every place that shaped him along the way.
THE THREE KNOCKOUTS THAT CHANGED EVERYTHING
Each of the three wins that turned Topuria into a global star topped the one before it, and watching them in sequence feels like watching someone speedrun their way to legendary status.
Alexander Volkanovski fell first at UFC 298 on February 17th, 2024, inside the Honda Center in Anaheim. Four years of featherweight dominance ended when Topuria caught him with a second-round knockout at the 3:32 mark. The California commission disclosed a flat purse of 350 thousand dollars plus a 50 thousand dollar bonus, and Topuria later revealed on the PBD Podcast that his total take came to 532 thousand \"all in,\" which is decent money but nowhere near superstar earnings for a title fight.
That financial picture shifted dramatically eight months later. UFC 308, October 26th, 2024, at the Etihad Arena in Abu Dhabi, featured Topuria against Max Holloway, and before the fight, Topuria made a prediction that nobody took seriously, declaring he would become the first man to knock Holloway out. Nobody had ever done it in Holloway's entire career. A right cross wobbled Holloway, a left hook put him on the canvas, and ground-and-pound sealed the finish in round three at the 1:34 mark, making good on the exact promise.
Topuria's words after the fight carried genuine emotion, quote, \"To beat a legend like Max Holloway, I don't know what to say, he inspired me so much my whole career.\" Holloway called it \"a perfect shot\" and credited Topuria as \"just the better man tonight,\" which showed real class from both sides. On the PBD Podcast, Topuria put his earnings for that fight at about 2.4 million dollars.
Recognition piled up fast after that performance. UFC dot com named him 2024 Fighter of the Year alongside Alex Pereira, and ESPN, CBS, Sporting News, MMA Junkie, and Yahoo Sports all handed him the same award independently.
Charles Oliveira completed the trilogy of former champions at UFC 317 on June 28th, 2025, inside the T-Mobile Arena in Las Vegas, and this one barely lasted long enough to settle into your seat. Topuria put Oliveira away at the 2:27 mark of round one, earning the vacant lightweight title and becoming the UFC's 10th multi-division champion and the first to capture two belts while carrying an undefeated record. Oliveira admitted afterward that Topuria hit him harder than anyone ever had, quote, \"definitely the one who hit me the hardest.\" The pound-for-pound number one ranking followed immediately.
Three fights, three former champions, three knockouts, all within sixteen months. The Conor McGregor comparisons spread everywhere, and at the time, the comparison held up.
Topuria's departure from featherweight had been announced on February 19th, 2025, with Dana White pointing to weight-cut difficulties, a shallow pool of appealing contenders at 145, and Topuria's own desire to compete at lightweight permanently. A medical issue connected to his weight cut had already forced him off UFC 270 back in January 2022, so everyone understood why the move needed to happen.
At that point, nobody could see what was about to go wrong.
THE PERSONAL CRISIS
November 2025 brought the first sign of trouble when Topuria ruled himself out of fighting in the first quarter of 2026 without offering a clear reason.
Clarity arrived on December 15th, 2025, through an Instagram statement where Topuria claimed someone had threatened to \"disseminate false allegations of domestic abuse unless financial demands were met.\" The allegations, in his words, were \"entirely unfounded,\" and he stated that evidence including \"audio recordings, written communications, witness statements, and video material\" had already been turned over to the judicial authorities.
Spanish newspaper El Mundo reported that Topuria received a summons to Spain's Court of Violence against Women on January 7th, 2026, which Topuria described as a family and administrative matter related to his daughter's ability to travel outside Spain. His divorce filing against Giorgina Uzcategui dated back to late October 2025, and they share two children together, a son born in 2019 and a daughter born in 2024.
Full transparency here, because it matters: no public resolution of either the abuse allegations or the extortion claim has surfaced as of this recording, meaning both remain open and unresolved, and we're presenting only what's been publicly stated without drawing any conclusions in either direction.
What we can say with certainty is that the pound-for-pound number one fighter in the world sat on the sidelines during what should have been the peak stretch of his career. Islam Makhachev reclaimed the top pound-for-pound spot on November 19th, 2025, and Topuria couldn't answer it.
THE INTERIM BELT AND THE COACHING SPLIT
Topuria's absence forced the UFC to create an interim lightweight title, and Justin Gaethje captured it by beating Paddy Pimblett via unanimous decision, scored 48-47, 49-46, 49-46, at UFC 324 on January 24th, 2026, a card that also served as the first event under the UFC's seven-year, 7.7 billion dollar deal with Paramount.
Another disruption had already taken root behind the scenes. Back in May 2025, just weeks before the Oliveira knockout, Topuria and his brother Aleksandre walked away from Jorge and Agustín Climent after 13 years of training together at Climent Club in Alicante, with both sides calling the separation mutual and pointing to the brothers' move to Madrid as the reason.
That timing meant Topuria entered the biggest fight of his career operating under a brand new setup called \"Topuria Team,\" headquartered in Madrid, with a roster that included his brother Aleks, boxing coach Javi Climent, who despite sharing the surname never had any connection to Climent Club, along with Mani Tavanaei, Mathias Ribeiro, and physical trainer Jesús Gallo.
Both Chael Sonnen and Daniel Cormier publicly questioned whether abandoning a 13-year coaching relationship right before a superfight amounted to a smart decision. Given what happened next, that skepticism aged well.
March 7th, 2026 brought the official announcement of the unification bout, and the venue chosen for it had never hosted anything like a UFC fight before.
UFC FREEDOM 250: THE NIGHT IT ALL FELL APART
June 14th, 2026, on the South Lawn of the White House, on Donald Trump's 80th birthday, billed as part of the U.S. 250th-anniversary celebrations. Fox News reported an estimated 4,300 in attendance including around 1,200 active-duty service members, while WTOP reported another 80,000 tickets allotted for viewing screens out on the Ellipse.
Nothing in UFC history looked like this event.
Topuria entered as an overwhelming favorite, with CBS Sports and SportsLine listing him at -500 on fight day against Gaethje's +380, and BetMGM's fight-week line stretching even wider to -555 against +400. The betting market treated this like a coronation, not a contest.
Two rounds of action backed up that confidence entirely. Topuria scored the only knockdown of the entire fight and landed the only takedown, taking full mount in round two and connecting on 44 of 60 significant strikes in that period alone. Complete dominance.
And that's when he made the choice that cost him everything. Yahoo Sports identified the exact turning point: after hurting Gaethje, Topuria followed him to the ground, took mount, and burned energy chasing several unsuccessful submission attempts instead of finishing a wounded opponent on the feet, which is the exact skill that defined his entire career. Gaethje survived the round, recovered, and the fight changed permanently from that moment forward.
Topuria's output collapsed in rounds three and four while Gaethje's head-hunting wore him down strike by strike. All three scorecards read 39 to 37 in Gaethje's favor when Topuria's corner stepped in and stopped the fight after round four ended.
The total strike count tells a tighter story than the finish suggests. F4W and MMAIndex through UFCStats recorded Topuria outlanding Gaethje 97 to 91 in significant strikes, while ESPN scored it at 91 each. Every source agreed that Topuria earned the only knockdown and the only takedown. None of that mattered because the narrative wrote itself: a -500 favorite quit on the stool.
Topuria's injury list revealed how savage those final two rounds truly were. Both orbital bones fractured, the right one worse than the left, along with a broken nose, a broken septum, and a broken foot. His own description of the damage, directed at Gaethje, carried real weight, quote, \"You took the sight from my right eye in the first round, and by the end of the second, from my left too.\" Medical officials handed down the maximum 180-day suspension, though no surgery proved necessary, and team member Iñigo Perez confirmed Topuria left the hospital the following day.
The card itself set a record that may never be matched, with all seven bouts ending by KO or TKO, a first in UFC history. Ciryl Gane stopped Alex Pereira in round two of the co-main to claim the interim heavyweight title, and Sean O'Malley, Bo Nickal, and Josh Hokit all picked up wins as well. Bonus payouts jumped to 400 thousand for Fight of the Night and 425 thousand for Performance of the Night, and Gaethje alone collected 825 thousand dollars in bonus money.
Viewership reached staggering levels: 17 million total across the U.S. and Latin America, 15.26 million within the U.S. alone, with a 7 million domestic average that set the record for the most-watched UFC event in American history, and an estimated 34 million worldwide according to the UFC's own release.
Even those numbers carried a footnote, though. Front Office Sports pointed out that the viewership \"still falls well short of prior expectations of a 'Super Bowl-type' audience.\" Dana White's own reaction told you everything about the economics, quote, \"There's no way we can do this again, I can't afford it.\"
THE MESSY AFTERMATH
Two distinct versions of Topuria emerged after that loss, and they tell very different stories about where his head landed.
Version one appeared immediately after the fight, and it sounded like a true champion, quote, \"No excuses, I had one of the best camps of my life, we will have our rematch.\" Clean, respectful, exactly what a defeated titleholder should say when the cameras are rolling.
Version two surfaced in September, and the tone had shifted completely. Medical clearance arrived on September 16th, marked by a post reading \"Final medical tests done, I'm back,\" which landed well enough on its own. What followed did not. Topuria floated the idea that Gaethje had applied \"anything weird\" to his gloves, and the full quote left no ambiguity about his state of mind: \"Justin, f*** you, and Ali, f*** you too,\" referring to Gaethje's manager Ali Abdelaziz.
Zero commissions raised any concern about the gloves, no UFC official addressed it, and no evidence of tampering exists in any form. Gaethje's comeback line landed perfectly, quote, \"People saying I've got bricks in my gloves, what a compliment.\"
The criticism snowballed from there. Arman Tsarukyan and Islam Makhachev both targeted Topuria publicly for quitting on the stool, and Gaethje shut down any rematch talk outright, quote, \"He doesn't get a rematch, he quit on the stool.\"
All of the goodwill earned by that gracious post-fight speech evaporated in a matter of weeks.
THE PADDY PIMBLETT SAGA
Running underneath all of this for five years has been a rivalry that refuses to die no matter how many times someone declares it over.
Topuria and Paddy Pimblett first clashed in 2021 over a Pimblett tweet aimed at Georgian fighter Guram Kutateladze, and by 2022 the feud had escalated to a physical altercation at a London hotel where hand-sanitizer bottles were flying through the air, which sounds made up but genuinely happened.
UFC 317 in June 2025 produced another confrontation when Joe Rogan called Pimblett into the cage after Topuria finished Oliveira, and Topuria responded by shoving him on live television with his wife and child standing right there in the Octagon. Dana White's assessment of that moment came down to one sentence: it \"should have never happened.\"
Topuria later stated publicly that the personal beef between them had ended, that he'd moved past it and closed the chapter.
That declaration aged about seventeen months before expiring. On November 5th, 2026, Topuria called Pimblett a \"sausage\" he would \"eat,\" which tells you exactly how resolved this rivalry actually is.
THE COMEBACK: WHAT WE ACTUALLY KNOW
Separating confirmed facts from speculation matters here because the noise level around this comeback is already deafening.
On the confirmed side: Topuria received full medical clearance on September 16th, 2026, and has split his training between Miami and Georgia, working alongside boxer Lasha Guruli and grappling coach Beka Guruli. November 3rd brought a post from the Didgori memorial in Georgia, a site commemorating a famous Georgian military victory, with the caption reading, \"I returned to where it all began, the Matador is coming back.\"
A gap exists between Topuria and his own team on timing. Physical trainer Jesús Gallo publicly stated that Topuria would reach fight-readiness \"starting from February,\" while Topuria himself contradicted that on November 5th by targeting April or later, putting the fighter and his trainer on different pages in front of everyone.
Dana White's contribution amounts to \"early 2027\" with no opponent attached, adding, quote, \"It drives me mental.\"
The realistic path forward looks clear enough. Gaethje refuses the rematch outright. Arman Tsarukyan earned the next title shot by knocking out Mauricio Ruffy at UFC 331. An immediate route back to the belt doesn't exist for Topuria right now.
Paddy Pimblett stands out as the most commercially logical matchup. Pimblett is currently sidelined for stem-cell treatment on a timeline that aligns with Topuria's April-or-later target, making that fight the single most probable booking even though nothing has been made official. PFL champion Usman Nurmagomedov has also publicly called Topuria out, adding another name to the list of possibilities.
THE BUSINESS SIDE
The financial and branding picture around Topuria explains why the UFC wants this comeback to happen sooner rather than later.
Approximately 19.6 million followers sat on his Instagram account as of late 2026 according to HypeAuditor, a number that made him the most-followed active UFC fighter after he passed Islam Makhachev in late 2025. During Freedom 250 fight week alone, the UFC reported that Topuria gained 2 million followers, dwarfing Gaethje's 420 thousand over the same period.
That follower count deserves a caveat. Fans have repeatedly accused Topuria of purchasing followers after suspicious overnight spikes, with one reported jump of 700 thousand in a single night consisting largely of accounts with no posts, and his team has never addressed the accusations.
Off the platform, his business interests have expanded considerably. A shareholder stake in Madrid's WOW FC promotion gained serious visibility when Cristiano Ronaldo joined as a fellow shareholder on November 27th, 2025. Brand deals include a Dolce and Gabbana fragrance partnership for \"K by Dolce and Gabbana,\" endorsements with Venum and Bit2Me, his own merchandise store, and an NFL Madrid promotional appearance. A reported Richard Mille ambassadorship from March 2026 circulates online, though the sourcing on that comes from a weak aggregator and shouldn't be treated as confirmed.
Spain granted Topuria citizenship on March 5th, 2024, and Georgia followed by awarding him the Order of Honor on March 21st, 2024, with the Prime Minister personally guaranteeing he would retain his Georgian citizenship as well. His brother Aleksandre competes as a UFC bantamweight and faces Santiago Luna on November 21st, 2026.
One lingering ambition remains unfulfilled. At UFC 317, Topuria declared that a card at the Santiago Bernabéu in Madrid during 2026 was \"inevitable.\" That event never materialized, and until the UFC issues an actual announcement, the Bernabéu dream stays in the aspirational category.
WHERE THIS ALL LANDS
Seventeen wins, zero losses, three consecutive knockout victories over former champions, and the distinction of being the first undefeated two-division champion in UFC history. All of that belonged to Topuria before June 14th, 2026, and the Gaethje fight stripped away the thing that none of those stats can replace, which is the aura of invincibility. Two specific weaknesses surfaced that night: his cardio and output after an early burst of energy, and the amount of punishment his body can absorb across a longer fight at 155 pounds. Every future opponent now owns that blueprint.
Leaving the coaching team that built him after 13 years, enduring a divorce and an extortion accusation that played out in public view, absorbing fractures in both orbital bones alongside a broken nose, a broken septum, and a broken foot, and then torching the respect he'd earned with a gracious post-fight speech by floating a glove tampering theory backed by nothing, all of that baggage travels with him into whatever comes next.
None of that erases who this fighter proved he could be at his best. At 29 years old, just two years removed from systematically knocking out Volkanovski, Holloway, and Oliveira in sequence, something nobody else in UFC history managed while undefeated, Topuria's grappling pedigree remains world-class, his power remains undeniable, and his record still reads 17 and 1.
Training has resumed. Call-outs are flying. Dana White is working on the booking.
What nobody can predict is which version walks into the Octagon in 2027. Will it be the fighter who told the world he'd knock out Max Holloway and then delivered on that promise to the second, or the one who faded after two rounds and accused the man who beat him of cheating.
El Matador says he's returning, and early 2027 is the window. The Octagon will answer every question that matters.
