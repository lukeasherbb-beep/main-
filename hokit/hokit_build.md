Josh Hokit lost his heavyweight championship fight against Ciryl Gane at UFC three thirty-four by a single round on all three scorecards. Before Bruce Buffer had even finished reading the decision, Jon Jones was filming himself telling Hokit to put his hand down. This is the story of the fight that broke a heavyweight record at Madison Square Garden, and the online war it started between the man many call the greatest of all time and the loudest man in the UFC. [FX: chapter-number — 01 · THE FINGER]
[CLIP: #2 Hokit flips off Buffer during the introduction]
On November fourteenth, Hokit walked out as the challenger in his first title fight, and the night started ugly before a single punch was thrown. Buffer had said a month earlier that he didn't like what Hokit talks about, calling him disrespectful and telling him to show some class. When the most famous voice in the sport stood in the cage and called his name, Hokit put his middle finger right in front of Buffer's face, something Dana White says nobody had ever done before.

[CLIP: #3 Round 1 Hokit rushes in, wild hooks]
[FE CARD: eye poke still (Hokit's Instagram screenshot, user-supplied) — gold frame]
[LET IT PLAY: #4lip Round 1 single-leg slam — 6s, arena audio]
Then the bell rang, and Hokit turned a title fight into a bar fight. He shot for takedowns from the opening second, swung wild hooks in bunches, and never gave the champion a single inch of room. Early in the first round, Gane's fingers caught him in the left eye, the same kind of foul that ended Gane's fight with Tom Aspinall in a no contest a year earlier. Referee Marc Goddard let it go, with no warning and no point taken. Hokit kept coming anyway, and late in that round he picked Gane up on a single leg and slammed him to the canvas.

[FE CARD: S("ROUND 3", [("71-37", "Total strikes, Hokit"), ("2-2", "Judges after 4 rounds")]) — gold]
[CLIP: #5 Round 3 Hokit flips Gane off at the horn]
For three rounds the pace looked impossible for a heavyweight. In the third, Hokit out-landed Gane seventy-one to thirty-seven in total strikes, Gane's corner was begging him to rest, and Hokit finished the round by flipping the champion off twice. Going into the fifth, the judges had it dead even at two rounds apiece.

[CLIP: #6 Rounds 4-5 Gane jabs and uppercuts, Hokit tired]
[FE CARD: S("HEAVYWEIGHT RECORD", [("237", "Gane significant strikes"), ("11 / 11", "Takedowns stuffed late"), ("158-84", "Final 10 minutes")]) — gold]
What nobody saw coming was how the fight would end. Gane found a second wind in the championship rounds, stuffed all eleven of Hokit's takedown attempts, and out-struck an exhausted challenger one hundred fifty-eight to eighty-four over the final ten minutes. By the last bell, Gane had landed two hundred thirty-seven significant strikes, a new single-fight UFC heavyweight record, passing the mark held by Daniel Cormier. Together the two men set the record for the most combined significant strikes in heavyweight history.

[CLIP: #7 Buffer fake-out, Gane's hand raised]
[FE CARD: S("OFFICIAL", [("48-47", "x3, Gane"), ("10-1", "Hokit's new record")]) — dark]
[CAPTION: DID HOKIT GET ROBBED?]
That's when Buffer got his revenge. Reading the decision, he dragged out the word "and," leaned toward Hokit as if the next word would be "new," then spun around and shouted "still." Forty-eight, forty-seven on all three cards, Gane retained, and Hokit's perfect ten and oh record was gone. For my money, Buffer's fake-out was the cleanest shot anyone landed on Hokit all night. Here's the question everyone's arguing about. Did Josh Hokit get robbed at Madison Square Garden? Tell me in the comments.

[FX: chapter-number — 02 · SAME COACHES]
[CLIP: #8 Jones' Instagram video "Put your hand down" (vertical)]
[FE CARD: quote "Same coaches different results." — Jon Jones, Instagram]
While Hokit stood in the cage with his hand half raised, Jon Jones had his phone out. The video he posted to Instagram has him saying, "Put your hand down. Put your hand down," then laughing once the result is read, and finishing with, "Good job, Frenchman." The caption was four words long. "Same coaches different results."

[CLIP: #9 Jones submits Gane, UFC 285, March 2023]
[FE CARD: T("JACKSON WINK MMA") — Albuquerque, Greg Jackson · Mike Winkeljohn]
To understand why that line stings, you need to know where both men train. Jackson Wink MMA in Albuquerque is the gym that built Jones into a two-division champion, and in two thousand twenty-three Jones submitted Gane in the first round at UFC two eighty-five to win the heavyweight belt. Hokit trains at that same gym today, under Greg Jackson and Mike Winkeljohn, and Winkeljohn is the coach who barred Jones from his gym back in two thousand twenty-one after an arrest, ending a twelve-year relationship.

[CLIP: #10 Steveson KO loss, UFC 331 (12 s)]
[CLIP: #11 Hokit diss track / media-day clip]
The beef itself goes back to September. Gable Steveson, the Olympic gold medalist Jones mentors, got knocked out in twelve seconds at UFC three thirty-one, and Hokit told Ariel Helwani that Jones was to blame for not preparing him properly. Next came a diss track before UFC three thirty-four with personal lines aimed at Jones, and a promise that he'd refuse to shake his hand. I think Jones had been waiting two months for that Saturday night.

[FX: chapter-number — 03 · THE WAR OF WORDS]
[FE CARD: post card — Hokit: "Jon Jones moves like Sean Combs lol. #rentfree"]
[FE CARD: post card — Jones on X: "the craziest coincidence"]
Hokit was in the comments of that Instagram post almost immediately, writing that Jon Jones moves like Sean Combs, with the hashtag rent free. The next day Jones posted on X that fighters who disrespect him tend to lose their next fight, calling it the craziest coincidence. That lit the fuse. Hokit answered with a list of Jones' past legal charges and scandals, and from there the whole thing went off the rails.

[FE CARD: post card — Jones: "He's not on drugs, you idiot" (dark; do NOT show the teammate's name or photo)]
Hokit claimed a former teammate from the gym fell apart after getting involved with drugs while hanging around Jones. Jones fired back that the young man was never on drugs, and that he was the one who got him admitted to a hospital and paid for his flight home to Switzerland. An MMA agent publicly backed Jones' version, and none of Hokit's accusation has been proven. Out of everything in this feud, that one crossed a line, and dragging a private person into a Twitter fight helped nobody.

[FE CARD: post card — Jones: "most punches absorbed in UFC heavyweight history"]
[FE CARD: T("GOOD LUCK LITTLE MAN") — dark]
By Monday, Jones tried to close the book. He wrote that watching Hokit break the record for most punches absorbed in UFC heavyweight history at Madison Square Garden was entertaining enough, but recycling the same insults he'd been hearing for ten years was weak. "Say whatever you want about me," Jones added. "I'm still simply better than you." He signed off by saying it was his last time addressing Hokit until he gets to a certain level, and a four-word send-off. "Good luck little man."

[FE CARD: post card — "Don't get quiet in the championship rounds now, Josh." (DELETED stamp)]
It wasn't his last time. Four minutes later, Jones posted, then deleted, "Don't get quiet in the championship rounds now, Josh."

[CLIP: #12 Round 1 eye poke replay]
[FE CARD: post card — Jones: "you absolutely folded"]
The eye poke became the next battlefield. Jones told Hokit that everyone in this sport has been poked in the eye, and that Greg Jackson told him at the start of the fourth round he had ten minutes left to become a world champion. "And you absolutely folded," Jones wrote. "You can't blame that on an eye poke. You got picked apart." Hokit had already posted a screenshot of the foul, writing that he saw triple the whole fight.

[CLIP: #13 the single-leg slam replay]
[FE CARD: post card — Hokit: "Style of takedown matters"]
Even the takedowns turned into an argument. Jones said there are no style points for lifting someone high in the air and doing nothing once you're on top. Hokit quoted the UFC's own judging criteria in reply, saying style of takedown matters as well as impact of punches, but that he wouldn't expect MMA rules to matter to someone who doesn't follow the rules in our penal code.

[FE CARD: post card — Jones: "I guarantee you'll never see heavyweight Gold"]
[FE CARD: post card — Hokit: "Old, washed-up" (censored)]
[LET IT PLAY: #14lip Hokit post-fight / rematch callout clip — 10s]
Then Jones made a promise. "You can't go from being a punching bag to begging for a title shot," he wrote. "I guarantee you'll never see heavyweight gold. Mark my words." Hokit dared the thirty-nine-year-old to come out of retirement and whoop him himself, called him old and washed up, and said this new version of Gane would destroy Jones, and so would Tom Aspinall. Jones laughed that off and went somewhere personal. "Let's not pretend I didn't do to you in sparring what I did every single time," he wrote. "Greg saw it, and the whole team saw it."

[FX: chapter-number — 04 · THE REACTION]
Here's how the MMA world reacted, and the surprise is who ended up on Hokit's side.

[LET IT PLAY: #15lip Pereira backstage "I thought that was a robbery, man" — 12s]
[CLIP: #16 Pereira and Hokit backstage "For me, you won"]
Alex Pereira, a man Hokit has mocked for months and even flipped off at the UFC three thirty-four press conference, watched from the crowd and couldn't believe the cards. "I thought that was a robbery, man," Pereira said backstage. "I thought Hokit won the fight. Man, Gane didn't do anything. I wanted Ciryl Gane to win, but come on, not like that." Then he found Hokit backstage and told him, "Hey, bro. Chama, congrats, my brother. For me, you won." Hokit's reply was short and almost polite. "Best of luck in your fight, Chama."

[LET IT PLAY: #17lip Dana White presser "He's the real deal" — 10s]
[FE CARD: quote — Dana White: "Everything that guy does crosses a line."]
Dana White scored it four rounds to one for Gane, and still sounded like a convert. "The guy's got an unbelievable chin, unstoppable cardio, and I give him nothing but respect for the fight that he fought tonight," White said at the post-fight press conference. He even admitted he used to think Hokit was a big mouth goofball and hated his whole shtick. "He's the real deal," White said. On the Buffer finger, he was blunter. "Nobody has flipped Buffer off before," White said, before adding that "everything that guy does crosses a line."

[LET IT PLAY: #18lip Buffer to Jon Anik "There's kids watching" — 10s]
Buffer told Jon Anik he'd planned to slap the finger away and that Daniel Cormier talked him out of it. "There's kids watching," Buffer said. "He got his panties in a bunch over a little comment I made."

[FE CARD: post card — McGregor: "the biggest draw in the UFC today"]
[FE CARD: quote — Mike Winkeljohn, Submission Radio]
Conor McGregor went the other direction from Jones completely. "After the weekend's performance, and outside of myself, Josh Hokit is the biggest draw in the UFC today," McGregor wrote. Winkeljohn, the coach who has now trained both men, called Jones' behavior very disappointing. "Josh will be the best eventually," Winkeljohn said. "Right now, Jon Jones, I would still say, he's the best of all time."

[FX: chapter-number — 05 · WHAT'S NEXT]
[FE CARD: S("NEXT AT HEAVYWEIGHT", [("DEC 12", "Pereira vs Pavlovich, UFC 335"), ("?", "Volkov · Aspinall · Hokit")]) — gold]
All of this leaves Hokit in a strange spot. He's ten and one now, with one loss to the heavyweight champion of the world in a fight that set records. Right after the decision, he asked for an immediate rematch on Instagram, tagging Dana White, matchmaker Mick Maynard and executive Hunter Campbell. No rematch has been booked. Alexander Volkov is in line, Aspinall is out there if he's healthy, and Pereira fights Sergei Pavlovich at UFC three thirty-five on December twelfth in Las Vegas, with White saying a Pereira and Hokit fight depends on how that one goes.

[CLIP: #19 Hokit walkout / career montage (debut Nov 2025 to MSG)]
Jones, meanwhile, is retired and still the most talked-about heavyweight in the sport without throwing a punch. He said he was done addressing Hokit, then kept posting anyway. For my money, he isn't finished. Twelve months ago, Josh Hokit was making his UFC debut, and this week he has Jon Jones, Bruce Buffer, Dana White and Alex Pereira all talking about him, which might be exactly the point.
