Daniel Cormier just called out Jon Jones for kicking Josh Hokit while he was down after UFC three thirty-four, and he did it on his own YouTube channel for everyone to hear. In the same week, Ilia Topuria's ex-wife broke her silence on the divorce that kept him out of the octagon for almost a year, and she called it hell. If you're new here, subscribing to Fight Era helps the channel and costs you nothing.

Start with Cormier, because nobody in the sport knows Jon Jones like he does. The two fought twice for the light heavyweight title. Jones won the first one by unanimous decision at UFC one eighty-two in January two thousand fifteen. He knocked Cormier out in the rematch at UFC two fourteen in July two thousand seventeen, and that result got overturned to a no contest after Jones tested positive for a banned steroid called Oral Turinabol. Cormier got the belt back. The rivalry never really ended, and even after retiring in two thousand twenty, Cormier still calls Jones out whenever he thinks Bones is in the wrong.

When Jones spent the days after UFC three thirty-four mocking Hokit's loss to Ciryl Gane, Cormier had plenty to say. "Jon tends to come when he thinks that someone that has spoken in opposition of him is down," Cormier said. He went further, calling it disappointing that Jones attacks people time and time again, usually when they're already down, and pointing out that Hokit hadn't even mentioned Jones before the latest round started. "If you're retired, you don't pick fights with these guys," Cormier added, "because you're not going to fight them, and you have no intention to fight them."

That last part landed hard, because Hokit had just offered to settle the whole thing in the cage, daring the thirty-nine-year-old to come out of retirement, calling him old and washed up, and telling him to try and whoop him himself. Jones laughed it off, reminding him, "You literally just lost," and then told Hokit, "let's not start talking street stuff in Albuquerque. It's really not that serious, and you're not about that life." Cormier's verdict on all of it fit in one line. "We're talking about Jon Jones when it really isn't his story anymore."

This isn't a new pattern, either. After Ciryl Gane's eye poke ended Tom Aspinall's title fight in a no contest at UFC three twenty-one in October two thousand twenty-five, Jones went after Aspinall online. When Alex Pereira lost to Gane at the White House in June, Jones posted about him too, although those two later patched things up. Every time a rival stumbles, Jones seems to show up in the replies.

Here's the twist. Cormier isn't exactly Hokit's biggest fan either. A week before the fight, the two sat down together, and Cormier asked whether all the trash talk actually helps. Hokit brought up Cormier's old payday against Jones, guessing fifty thousand dollars, and Cormier corrected him to eighty thousand, back at the start of his UFC run. Hokit's answer was pure Hokit. "If I gotta crack a few eggs to make an omelette, that's what I'm gonna do."

There's no bad blood, though. "I do not hate Josh Hokit," he said. "Actually, Josh Hokit and I had a great conversation on Monday. I told him how proud of him I was because he's a wrestling guy and he represents a sport that I love more than anything in the world." He even agreed with the fans who said referee Marc Goddard should have taken a point from Gane for the first-round eye poke.

Fight night left Cormier taking heat of his own. Fans accused him of openly favoring Gane on the broadcast, and a petition to pull him off UFC commentary passed seven thousand signatures. Jones jumped on that too, posting that he had rewatched the fight "without the glazing commentary" and that it was clear who won, without ever naming Cormier. Jon Anik defended his partner.

For my money, Cormier is right on this one, even if he's the last person Jones wants to hear it from. A retired fighter who won't take the fight shouldn't be throwing the first punch online. Now to Ilia Topuria, whose year has been rough in a completely different way. This week his ex-wife, Giorgina Uzcategui, sat down on the El Cafetal podcast and talked publicly about their divorce. Asked about the hardest period of her life, she didn't hesitate. "Without a doubt, the divorce."

She described being in a country that wasn't her own, far from her family, with a one-year-old baby, while dealing with a media situation she called absolutely horrible. "Divorce is already a painful and complicated situation in itself," she said, "but I had so many other things going on that made it hell." She also said leaving was the right call. "In my case I think it was the best decision I could have made. Continuing the relationship would have meant compromising with myself in a way I couldn't accept." She didn't point to any specific incident, and she ended on something close to peace. "Regardless, I wish him the best because he is my daughter's father."

To understand why that interview matters, you have to go back a year and a half. In June two thousand twenty-five, Topuria knocked out Charles Oliveira at UFC three seventeen, becoming the first undefeated two-division champion in UFC history at seventeen and oh. A few months later, the marriage was over, and by November he had stepped away from defending his title.

In December two thousand twenty-five, the story turned ugly. Reports said his wife had filed a complaint accusing him of domestic and gender violence, and Topuria denied it completely. On December fifteenth he posted a statement on Instagram, saying he had faced "threats to disseminate false allegations of domestic abuse unless financial demands were met." He called the allegations "entirely unfounded," and added that "the truth is not a matter of opinion. It is a matter of evidence." According to Topuria, that evidence included audio recordings, messages, witness statements and video, and he said it had been handed to the courts.

In January, a Spanish court reportedly turned down her request to move to Miami with their daughter. In early February two thousand twenty-six, the two reached an out-of-court settlement. Madrid newspaper ABC reported that the agreement withdrew the domestic abuse complaint, and that she accepted the same terms Topuria had offered from the very beginning. Their daughter stays in Spain, Topuria kept his Madrid home and gym, and he was given what was described as a generous visitation schedule. No court ever ruled on the allegations themselves, and Topuria has never been charged with anything.

Topuria later described what that stretch did to him. "It's strange because, honestly, everything changed overnight," he said in an interview before his comeback. "It's changed me a lot." He framed it as a daily choice. "I can experience it negatively, or I can simply accept my reality and move forward," he said, and once the settlement was signed he went straight back into camp at his home gym in Madrid.

That cleared the way for his comeback, and the divorce followed him straight into fight week. Before their title fight at the White House, Justin Gaethje told Fox Sports Australia that Topuria was a gimmick, and then went personal. "All I can say is this. I would leave him." Topuria fired back on the UFC's fight-week series. "Actually, I thought he was a great guy," he said. "But since he crossed the personal line, he's not going to have my respect." He promised to put Gaethje to sleep in two minutes.

It didn't go that way. On June fourteenth, on the White House lawn, the thirty-seven-year-old Gaethje beat Topuria by a corner stoppage after the fourth round, handing him the first loss of his career and taking the undisputed lightweight title. Topuria then accused Gaethje of tampering with his gloves, a claim Gaethje called a lame excuse and the commission dismissed. This week, Topuria revealed that the orbital fractures from that fight came half a millimeter from ending his career, and Joe Rogan has said he should go back down to one forty-five.

I think the most telling part of her interview is the last line. After everything that played out in public, she still says she wishes him the best, and that's about as calm an ending as this story could have had. For Topuria, the next chapter is all about the cage again.
