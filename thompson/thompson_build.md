This is the story of Stephen Wonderboy Thompson, a forty-three-year-old karate fighter who had lost five of his last six fights, and the spinning wheel kick he landed at Madison Square Garden in the final fight of his career. It's also the story of a nickname, one that a fifteen-year-old from Simpsonville, South Carolina picked up in a kickboxing ring and carried for twenty-eight years.

[FX: territory-date — SIMPSONVILLE, SOUTH CAROLINA · 1998]
In nineteen ninety-eight, that kid stepped into the ring across from a twenty-six-year-old who was twenty and oh. Nobody gave him a chance. Thompson won decisively, and before the night was over the ring announcer had handed him the name he'd keep for the rest of his life.

Twenty-eight years later, on November fourteenth, two thousand and twenty-six, Wonderboy walked out for UFC three thirty-four knowing it was the last time. He went out the way he came in. With his feet.

[FX: chapter-number — 01 · THE KARATE KID]
Stephen Randall Thompson was born on February eleventh, nineteen eighty-three, into a house where fighting was basically the family trade. His father, Ray Thompson, is a former professional kickboxer who runs Upstate Karate in Simpsonville, and Ray also worked as his son's trainer and manager.
[FX: paired-profiles — CHRIS WEIDMAN (brother Tony's brother-in-law) · CARLOS MACHADO (sister Lindsay's husband, his BJJ coach)]
The family tree only gets more fight-heavy from there. His sister Lindsay married Carlos Machado, the jiu-jitsu coach who later trained Stephen, while his brother Tony married the sister of Chris Weidman, the former UFC middleweight champion.

[CLIP: #3 Young Thompson training at Upstate Karate (photo or footage)]
All those hours in his dad's school produced a style nobody his age could figure out, built on distance and speed, with kicks coming from angles opponents never saw until it was too late. That's how a fifteen-year-old ended up in a ring with a grown man. That's where Wonderboy starts.

[FE CARD: S("KICKBOXING", [("57-0", "Combined record"), ("37-0", "Amateur"), ("20-0", "Pro")]) — gold]

[FX: icon-bullets — 16 amateur titles · 2005 world champion · #1 World Combat League]
Sixteen amateur titles came with it, including a world championship in two thousand and five, and by two thousand and six he was ranked number one in the World Combat League, a kickboxing promotion founded by none other than Chuck Norris. His knockout total changes depending on which source you trust. The zero in the loss column never does.

[CLIP: #3 Kickboxing-era footage or photo]
There was nobody left to beat standing up. Thompson took the karate into mixed martial arts, won his first five pro fights, and got the call from the UFC.

[CLIP: #4 UFC 143 debut KO, Feb 2012]
February fourth, two thousand and twelve, UFC one forty-three. His octagon debut ended in a knockout. The second UFC fight didn't go his way, a decision loss, and honestly that loss is where the real career begins.

[CLIP: #7 Hendricks TKO, Feb 2016]
Johny Hendricks, a former welterweight champ, got stopped in the first round in February of two thousand and sixteen. Four months on, Thompson took a unanimous decision off Rory MacDonald.

[CLIP: #8 MacDonald striking exchanges, June 2016]
Seven in a row made him the number one contender, and on November twelfth, two thousand and sixteen, Thompson walked into Madison Square Garden to fight Tyron Woodley for the welterweight title.
[FX: chapter-number — 03 · MADISON SQUARE GARDEN]
Most people remember UFC two oh five as the night Conor McGregor knocked out Eddie Alvarez to become a two-division champion. The title fight further down that card deserves to be remembered just as much.

For long stretches Thompson picked at Woodley from the outside. The fourth round nearly ended everything. Woodley dropped him with right hands, wrapped up a guillotine, and squeezed for the better part of a minute, and somehow Thompson refused to tap, fought his way loose, and finished the round on top.

The next stretch was rougher. Darren Till beat him on the scorecards, and in two thousand and nineteen Anthony Pettis became the first man to knock him out.
